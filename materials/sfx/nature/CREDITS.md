## How these files were processed

Each source was cut, made loopable, gain-matched, and encoded. The exact steps are in `_tools/make_loop.py`:

1. **Cut** a clean stretch (the segment column above), avoiding obvious cars, voices, and mic bumps where we could.
2. **Seamless loop.** The 3 seconds after the loop end are equal-power crossfaded into the first 3 seconds, so you can lay the file end-to-end on a timeline (or loop it in a DAW) with no click or level dip. MP3 adds a few milliseconds of encoder padding, so if your editor shows a tiny gap at the seam, overlap the copies by 1 frame or add a 0.5 s crossfade.
3. **Gain.** One fixed gain per file, with no compression or limiting. Beds land at about **-24 LUFS integrated** with true peak at or below -1.5 dBFS. Peaky sources come out quieter so their peaks stay under that ceiling: fireplace about -34, wind_open-field about -31, rain_light-in-woods about -32, mojave about -26 LUFS. Turn those up in the mix as needed.
4. **Encode** to MP3 192 kbps CBR, 48 kHz stereo. Mono sources are dual-mono. 192k is transparent for noise-like ambience, especially once it's low-passed, and keeps the repo lean.

The root `.gitignore` keeps raw media out of git. `assets/sfx/nature/.gitignore` re-allows `*.mp3` **in this folder only**, for these curated, license-checked beds. Raw downloads and longer originals belong on the media drive, not here.

## Mixing tips for the "piano in another room" look

- **Two layers max under the piano.** For example: `rain_morning-through-open-window` plus `fireplace_crackling-wood-fire`, or `crickets_night-chorus-distant-surf` alone.
- **Filter the ambience differently from the piano.** The piano gets the heavy low-pass (about 1.5 to 3 kHz, "next room"). The weather is "outside the window", so filter it less (about 6 to 10 kHz) and keep it 6 to 12 dB under the piano. Rain that's duller than the piano sounds wrong.
- **Thunder is an event, not a bed.** Use `thunder_summer-storm-rolling` for a minute or two at a time over a steady rain bed, then let it pass.
- **Long videos (1 to 3 hours).** Loop the 2:30 files and offset two copies of the same rain by about 47 s at -6 dB each. The repetition becomes inaudible.

## Licensing notes

- **CC0 1.0**: a formal public-domain waiver by the author. No conditions.
- **Public domain** (Commons, from PDSounds): released into the public domain by the uploader or recordist.
- **Public Domain Mark 1.0** (radio aporee ::: maps on the Internet Archive): the recordist chose "public domain" as the license when uploading to radio aporee. Technically, PDM labels a work as free of known copyright rather than being a formal waiver like CC0. Since the recordist is the one who applied it, that's a clear public-domain dedication in practice. We keep the creator names and item links here as our record.
- **Not used:** BBC Sound Effects (RemArc, non-commercial only), anything CC-BY-SA, NC, or ND, and Freesound originals (Freesound's CDN blocks automated downloads from our tooling. The kvgarlic file above is the Commons mirror of a CC0 Freesound upload).

### If Content ID claims one of these

Public-domain nature recordings sometimes get claimed, because someone else registered a "rain sounds" album that uses the same free recording. A claim on one of these beds is almost always disputable. Dispute it as "public domain / CC0" and paste the source link from this file. Keep this file current whenever you add a sample.
